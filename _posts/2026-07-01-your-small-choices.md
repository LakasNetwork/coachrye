I started a new job in May. New office, new team, new rhythm. The kind of change worth telling people about.

But that's not really what stayed with me.

## When the pieces reshuffled

What stayed with me was smaller. A business idea I finally stopped circling. Some coaching engagements I declined. A financial course I quietly dropped because life got full and something had to give. Some friends I showed up for who didn't have to ask twice. Months of evening classroom and skills training sessions at the fire station, right up until the deputy handed us our pagers. 🚒

I'm not gonna lie. When you're navigating a big transition, it's easy to funnel all your attention onto the main event. The big decision. The new chapter. 

But what I realized is that who you actually are gets built in the margins.

In the weekend you show up at someone's door to help them move. In the no you finally say to the ideas and partnerships that never quite felt right. In the airport run you make for people you love. In the small commitments you keep showing up for, week after week, until one day someone calls you a firefighter.

Those aren't distractions from the main event. They are the main event. 🙌🏽

## A 15-second act

A few months back, I forwarded a job posting to an acquaintance, someone I'd met briefly at a seminar and crossed paths with again at church. Took maybe 15 seconds. I forgot I did it. He came up to me after mass one Sunday, wife beside him, to tell me he got the job. After almost half a year of unemployment.

Your small (heck, tiny) choices don't just shape who you're becoming. Sometimes they change someone else's story entirely.

Look at your small choices lately. Not your goals. Not your plans. Your actual choices.

They'll tell you more about who you're becoming than any goal-setting session ever could. 🎯
